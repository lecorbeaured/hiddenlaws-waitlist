Rich Dad Poor Dad vs Dave Ramsey: Debt-Free First, or Assets First?

Of every comparison on this list, this is the one where the two philosophies actually disagree instead of just emphasizing different things. Kiyosaki and Ramsey aren't circling the same idea from different angles — they give conflicting advice on the same decision.

Rich Dad Poor Dad: leverage and assets, debt can be a tool

Kiyosaki's framework treats debt as morally neutral — what matters is whether it's "good debt" (financing something that generates cash flow, like a rental property) or "bad debt" (financing consumption). The implication is that someone comfortable with leverage can use debt to acquire assets faster than someone who waits until they're debt-free.

Dave Ramsey (The Total Money Makeover): all debt is dangerous, eliminate it first

Ramsey's position is close to the opposite: no debt is good debt, and his entire system (the debt snowball, cash envelopes, the "baby steps") is built around becoming completely debt-free — including the mortgage — before anything else, investing included. Where Kiyosaki says leverage can accelerate you, Ramsey says leverage is what keeps most people stuck.

Which one is actually right for you?

Neither book is wrong — they're built for different starting points. If debt has been the thing sabotaging you, Ramsey's rigidity is the point: it removes the judgment calls Kiyosaki's framework requires. If you're already stable and want to move faster, Kiyosaki's approach to leverage makes more sense.

What both miss is the same thing: neither explains why some people handle debt and leverage calmly while others spiral with the exact same numbers on paper. That's a function of what you absorbed early about risk and money, not just which framework you pick.
